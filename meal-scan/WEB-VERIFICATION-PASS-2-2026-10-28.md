# Web Verification Pass 2 — 2026-10-28

Follow-up search prompted by Mark ("go out and do a web search, see if we should keep this thread live"). Result: all three terms left unverified in Pass 1 are now dictionary-confirmed, and one new lexical fact emerged. The thread can be closed; the archive stands.

## Upgrades

### ăn vã — VERIFIED (Vietnamese dictionaries + Wiktionary)
- VDict (Vietiet): "Ăn thức ăn mặn một mình, không ăn kèm với cơm hoặc các loại lương thực chính (như xôi, bánh mì, bún, phở)" — to eat salty/savory food alone, without rice or another staple; often outside the main meal.
- From điển tiếng Việt (loigiaihay): "Chỉ ăn thức ăn, không ăn với cơm" — to eat just the food, not with rice. Examples: ăn vã yogurt, sandwiches, dried rations on hikes.
- Wiktionary (English) matches the packet's working gloss EXACTLY: "to eat non-rice and non-vegetable-soup food (especially meat or fish) without rice during a rice meal."
- **Evaluative-markedness evidence (the packet asked for this):** Wiktionary cites the admonition "Ăn cả cơm chứ đừng ăn vã!" — "Eat your rice too, don't just eat vã!" The departure is mildly judged in usage — a caretaker correction, not stigma. A Vietnamese language column (thethaovanhoa.vn) also documents real usage ("Chờ cơm mãi chả thấy, cô nàng cứ thế ăn vã hết đĩa rau") while noting the vã element's formal dictionary status is unsettled.
- Nuance found: near-synonyms ăn chơi (recreational eating) and ăn không (alternate way to say eating without rice); vã itself also means to splash/sweat profusely/idle-talk — the eating sense is one branch of a verb about bare/undirected activity. (Observation, not etymology — firewall stands.)

### meratah — VERIFIED (Indonesian Wiktionary, KBBI-cited)
- "makan lauk saja (tidak dengan nasi)" — to eat just the lauk, not with rice. Confirmed as a real KBBI-linked verb (me- + ratah).

### menggado — VERIFIED + NEW LEXICAL FACT
- KBBI: "memakan lauk-pauk begitu saja (tentang ikan, daging, dan sebagainya) tidak dengan nasi" — to eat dishes (fish, meat, etc.) just like that, without rice.
- **NEW FACT: KBBI dictionaries list menggado and meratah as SYNONYMS.** The packet's open question "menggado vs meratah nuance" now has a dictionary answer: no lexical distinction. Any felt difference between them would be usage/register, not meaning.
- The gado-gado dish connection remains plausible folk-usage, but the dictionary verb stands on its own.

## Supporting usage evidence for the rice-as-meal institution (Indonesian)
- Indonesian web writing confirms the meal-constitution frame the packet hypothesized: "Yang namanya makan, ya makan nasi. Kalau belum makan nasi, itu namanya belum makan" ("Eating means eating rice. If you haven't eaten rice, you haven't eaten") — hipwee.com; and "MAKAN tanpa nasi belumlah disebut makan" ("eating without rice isn't yet called eating") — kabarbelanda.com, which also titled the piece "No Rice No Glory."
- This is usage evidence (blog register), not lexical fact — but it is exactly the speaker-side confirmation the packet's firewall demanded before promoting any "meal = rice" institution claim for Indonesian.
