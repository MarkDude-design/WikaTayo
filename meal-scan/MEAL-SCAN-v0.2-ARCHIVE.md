# PART 1 — Original packet (verbatim)

WIKA TAYO — CROSS-LANGUAGE MEAL INSTITUTION SCAN
REVIEW PACKET v0.2

PURPOSE

Examine several compact food expressions that appear to preserve an expected
meal structure.

Do not begin by accepting our interpretation.
First establish what the expressions ordinarily mean, how speakers use them,
and what emotional or evaluative weight they commonly carry.

CURRENT TERMS

TAGALOG / FILIPINO
papak / papakin
Working gloss: To eat ulam or another food by itself, especially without rice.

INDONESIAN
menggado
Working gloss: To eat lauk, such as meat or fish, without rice.

INDONESIAN
meratah
Working gloss: To eat lauk or another accompanying food by itself, without rice.

VIETNAMESE
ăn vã
Working gloss: To eat non-rice food—especially meat or fish—without rice during a rice meal.

THAI
กินข้าว — kin khao
Working gloss: Literally "eat rice"; commonly "eat" or "have a meal."

THAI
กับข้าว — kap khao
Working gloss: Literally "with rice"; food or dishes eaten with rice.

OBSERVED SHAPE

Several terms appear to preserve a configuration resembling:
rice or staple + food organized in relation to that staple = ordinary meal.
Some expressions compactly name what happens when the relational food is
eaten without the expected staple. Thai and Vietnamese also provide examples
in which the word for rice can expand toward the meal as an event or institution.

QUESTIONS (per term):
1. Most ordinary contemporary meaning?
2. Is eating without rice merely described, or marked as unusual?
3. What travels with the term: snacking / tasting / impatience / indulgence /
   scarcity / impropriety / incompleteness / humor / affection / no consistent judgment?
4. Inferior configuration or only an alternate one?
5. Does the accompanying food temporarily become the center of the event?
6. Does it remain conceptually classified in relation to the absent staple?
7. Which conclusions are lexical facts, usage tendencies, cultural
   interpretations, or structural inferences?
8. Where do these languages materially diverge despite apparent similarity?

DISTINCTIONS TO PRESERVE: structural markedness vs evaluative markedness vs emotional residue.

WORKING HYPOTHESES — DO NOT ASSUME
- ABSENT ORGANIZER: an expected element may continue organizing interpretation even while materially absent.
- LEXICAL INSTITUTION: a compact linguistic form may preserve roles, expectations, permissions, or violations within a recurring social practice.
- MEAL CONSTITUTION: a meal vocabulary may assign relational offices (staple, accompaniment, preparation, eater, host, guest, ordinary configuration, marked departure).

PROVISIONAL FORMULATION: A word can be a very small institution.

USEFUL ANALOGY — NOT EVIDENCE: A regent occupies the center while the sovereign is absent,
but the regent's office remains defined by that absence.

COMEDIC SHORTHAND: The ulam has seized the throne. The language recognizes the government
but withholds diplomatic approval.

FIREWALLS
- Similar usage does not prove shared ancestry.
- Shared ancestry does not prove direct transmission between these terms.
- Vietnamese may provide cultural or ecological convergence without shared Austronesian linguistic inheritance.
- Indonesian provides regional and language-family adjacency, not proof of a direct Philippine route.
- Thai may offer transparent structural comparison without proving borrowing.
- Dictionary meaning is not emotional residue.
- Structural markedness is not necessarily negative judgment.
- A neat parallel is not automatically a historical relationship.
- Do not promote "inferior state" unless speaker evidence supports it.
- Chữ Nôm resemblance or component analysis must not establish etymology by appearance alone.

HISTORICAL-SCRIPT NOTE
Vietnamese ăn has attested Chữ Nôm forms including 咹 and 𩛖. A historical Nôm character for the specific vã in ăn vã has not yet been verified. Do not manufacture one. Historical Vietnamese → Chữ Nôm → Chinese components → historical Chinese sound comparison is a preserved future research branch, not part of this pass.

REQUESTED OUTPUT
1. Correct or qualify the working glosses. 2. Report ordinary usage and common emotional/evaluative associations. 3. Separate lexical fact / usage evidence / historical evidence / structural inference / speculative interpretation. 4. Strongest genuine parallel. 5. Most important divergence. 6. Best mechanism name. 7. Return unresolved questions without forcing a grand theory.

# PART 2 — Z's review (web-research pass, 2026-10-28)

Note: this pass went beyond the packet's original "no external research" condition, and its results qualify the first pass. Glosses verified below in PART 3.

## Per-term findings

### papak / papakin (Tagalog)
- Lexical fact (dictionary-confirmed): to eat food, especially viands/ulam, without rice or bread.
- Usage evidence: everyday, informal; contexts like eating fried chicken by itself. Neutral in the dictionaries; the snacking/casual/humor coloring is a usage tendency, not part of the lexical meaning.
- No supported "inferior" reading: the sources describe an alternate configuration, not a lesser one.

### menggado (Indonesian)
- Attested uses tie menggado closely to gado-gado (the mixed salad eaten without rice); the more general "eat lauk without rice" verb reading is plausible but the first pass's scarcity/impropriety lean is NOT supported by available sources.
- Treat the scarcity reading as speculative until native-speaker evidence arrives.

### meratah (Indonesian)
- Not clearly documented in the English-language sources available this pass. Gloss remains working-only; possibly more neutral than menggado (distinction unverified).

### ăn vã (Vietnamese)
- Not verifiable in the English-language sources available this pass; Vietnamese-language sources are needed. All connotation claims (scarcity/impropriety/humor) remain unverified. The Nôm firewall stands: no Nôm form for vã was found, none was manufactured.

### kin khao กินข้าว (Thai)
- Confirmed: "eat rice" functions as the default "eat / have a meal"; unmarked. Rice as implicit meal organizer.

### kap khao กับข้าว (Thai)
- Confirmed: "with rice" = accompaniment dishes; unmarked.
- Related term surfaced by the pass: kap klaem — food eaten as companion to drinking. A THIRD relational frame (rice-companion vs drink-companion), useful for the cookbook split (PART 4).

## Strongest genuine parallel
papak / menggado / meratah / ăn vã (as working glosses) all name accompaniment-eaten-without-staple, all mark the departure, and all preserve the relational classification — the food stays conceptually "with rice" even when rice is absent. The web pass confirmed the Tagalog member of this family; the others remain pending.

## Most important divergence
Thai has the most transparent staple/accompaniment vocabulary (kin khao / kap khao) yet no dedicated "without rice" verb surfaced this pass. The culture that most explicitly names the rice-meal may have the least lexical need to name leaving it. This is a usage-gap observation, not a cultural claim.

## Mechanism name
"Lexical institution" remains the best fit (a compact form preserving roles, expectations, permissions, and violations within a recurring practice). "Meal constitution" is a close second for the fuller office-assignment picture. The packet's provisional formulation survived the review pass: a word can be a very small institution.

## Evidence separation (unchanged in kind, updated in content)
- Lexical facts: the confirmed meanings of papak/papakin, kin khao, kap khao.
- Usage evidence: casual/snacking contexts for papak; greeting-meal uses of kin khao.
- Historical evidence: still essentially none for the marked-eating verbs; family/adjacency claims untouched.
- Structural inference: absent-organizer, lexical-institution, meal-constitution.
- Speculative: scarcity/impropriety readings for menggado and ăn vã; the regent analogy; any "inferior configuration" claim.

# PART 3 — Gloss verification status (after web pass)

1. papak/papakin — CONFIRMED as the ordinary neutral verb "eat (food/viand) without rice or bread." Casual/humor tone = usage tendency, not lexical.
2. menggado — QUALIFIED. Tied to gado-gado in attested use; general verb reading plausible; scarcity/impropriety lean unsupported. Do not promote.
3. meratah — UNVERIFIED in available sources. Working gloss only.
4. ăn vã — UNVERIFIED in available English-language sources. Vietnamese-language research needed. Nôm rule stands.
5. kin khao — CONFIRMED. "Eat rice" = default "have a meal"; unmarked.
6. kap khao — CONFIRMED. "With rice" = accompaniments; unmarked. Bonus find: kap klaem (drink-companion frame).
