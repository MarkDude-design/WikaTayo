# Wika Tayo human review, wordplay, and poetry packet v0.1

## Purpose

Provide the smallest reusable method for testing language fragments without confusing model fluency, participant recognition, grammatical naturalness, personal resonance, and cultural authority.

## Intake gates

1. **Candidate fertilizer** — wide admission; may be wrong, strange, or model-generated.
2. **Human playtest material** — bounded set with provenance and a reason for testing.
3. **Published/core material** — native-speaker and practitioner review, provenance, consent, and rights checks complete.

## Reaction card

Allow multiple reactions:

```text
SINASABI KO
KILALA KO
NATUWA AKO
ANO BA ITO?
```

Provisional fifth response:

```text
TULOY KO
```

Do not force a participant to select only one.

## Record fields

```text
card
reaction
spoken_repair
alternate_order
new_completion
recognition_channel
personal_story_or_association
returned_to_later
speaker_initials_or_anonymous_code
consent_to_preserve_quote
consent_to_publish
reviewer_notes
```

`returned_to_later` is included because delayed return may carry more evidence than an immediate numerical score. It must not be overinterpreted.

## Evidence distinctions

- **Natural:** the speaker would plausibly say it.
- **Recognized:** the speaker knows or can reconstruct it.
- **Resonant:** it evokes affection, memory, humor, identity, or story.
- **Mechanically legal:** it fits the visible game rule.
- **Culturally reviewed:** an appropriate reviewer has examined its use and framing.

None of these automatically implies the others.

## Poetry method

Preserve fragments before expansion:

```text
gather pieces
→ mark provenance and certainty
→ find the smallest core
→ test productive silence
→ add only necessary bridges
→ offer limited variants
→ let the writer recognize the poem
```

Possible variant names:

- **Bare** — smallest skeleton.
- **Breathing** — spacing, rhythm, and minimal bridges.
- **Bloom** — fuller development without hiding authorship.

The system must distinguish:

- Mark’s words;
- another participant’s words;
- model-proposed material;
- grammatical bridges;
- later editorial changes.

## Working poem

```text
SABI KO,
“IKAW.”

SABI MO,
“TAYO.”

KILALA KITA.
NATUWA AKO.

ANO BA ITO?

PAG-IBIG.
```

Human response preserved with the source project:

```text
OO,
PARANG PAG-IBIG.
```

Public use of the surrounding relational story requires separate consent review.

## Word-game principles

- A blank may be a persistent slot, not empty space.
- A player-supplied completion may be more meaningful than the prepared answer.
- Rhythm may restore more than letters.
- The same card can support obvious, alternative, chaos, and Outlaw lanes.
- A spoken repair is a contribution, not merely a failed answer.
- The table can author new material.

## Physical test

1. Print a small mixed candidate set.
2. Show one card without explanation.
3. Allow recognition, completion, repair, gesture, or silence.
4. Add one cue only if needed.
5. Record exact responses and consent status.
6. Ask what the person noticed before explaining the design.
7. Preserve counterexamples and irritation.
8. Do not promote material during the session.

## Cautions

- No surprise testing of Filipino participants.
- No treating laughter as consent.
- No claiming a person represents all speakers.
- No public relational anecdote without approval.
- No historical inference from a successful modern game mechanic.
- No model-generated Baybayin treated as practitioner-reviewed.
